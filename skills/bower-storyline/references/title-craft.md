# Action Title Craft — rules, failure theory, procedure

Policy-resolved distillate — the **canonical copy** of the title rules. Other skills reference
this file; none duplicates it. Calibration corpus: `title-bank.md` (load it whenever composing
or judging titles).

## Why titles go bland — four mechanisms, four different fixes

1. **Written from the slide, not the argument.** "Q2 Revenue by Segment" summarizes the slide;
   "Enterprise carried the entire Q2 beat; SMB shrank a third quarter" summarizes its role in
   the argument. You cannot write the second without the whole ladder. → Titles are written at
   storyline time, top-down, never re-derived per slide.
2. **The intellectually blank assertion.** "There are three options" / "Key considerations" —
   states the *kind* of idea instead of the idea. It is a symptom of **incomplete thinking**,
   and it hides findings (Minto's case: "two organization problems" concealed "the company
   cannot delegate authority"). → **Route upstream for more thinking. NEVER hand to a rewriter
   — that produces a fancier blank assertion.**
3. **Blank verbs with no end product.** "Strengthen regional effectiveness." Test: *visualize a
   real person completing the action — what are they holding?* Nothing → rewrite ("Assign
   planning responsibility to the regions"). Specificity threshold: not "improve profits" but
   "improve profits by 10% by January 15" — a 2% and a 10% improvement demand different actions.
4. **Describing the subject, not what matters about it.** "Profits by Region" lets the reader
   pick the wrong message. "North generates the smallest share of profits" removes the risk.
   Same trend line supports four true messages — the title picks the one you mean.

## The rules

| # | Rule | Lint? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete declarative sentence with a finite verb | ✔ |
| 2 | One proposition — no "and" joining two claims | ✔ (heuristic) |
| 3 | Falsifiable — someone could disagree | judged |
| 4 | Specific — contains the number or the proper noun | ✔ |
| 5 | ≤2 lines in the title bar; 1 preferred; never shrink type to fit | ✔ |
| 6 | Present tense, active voice — **default register, not a lint**: real McKinsey titles include "Losses have been driven by…". Flag only when the passive hides the actor | judged |
| 7 | Sentence case; NO emphasis inside a title — no bold, colour, italics, caps, underline | ✔ |
| 8 | Every number in the title appears identically in the body | ✔ |
| 9 | No slop lexicon: unlock, leverage, journey, robust, holistic, empower, transformative, landscape, delve, synergy, seamless, best-in-class | ✔ |
| 10 | No general truths ("managing risk is important"), no absolutes ("all divisions fail…") | judged |
| 11 | Results, not process — never "we conducted 15 interviews" | judged |

**Judgement bar on top of the rules:** carries the so-what, not just the what · synthesises
("conversion fell 55→40%, likely driven by sales-team scaling"), never summarises ("we win 40%
of negotiations") · points toward action or decision · not obvious to a CEO · independent —
no "(I)/(II)" repeats; genuine continuations use the ellipsis form ("…should pursue volume
boosting initiatives…" / "…by taking critical actions leading to ~$2bn by 2020").

## The improvement ladder — climb every title to rung 4

1. Topic label — "Market dynamics"
2. Generic claim — "Focus on expansion to attractive geographies"
3. Specific but wordy — "We should prioritise APAC and NA because margins, growth, size…"
4. **Concise, quantified insight — "Asia Pacific and North America prioritized due to margins,
   growth and 100+ EUR mn market size"**

## Title grammars — shapes to pour claims into

| Shape | Template |
|---|---|
| Driver attribution | *[Outcome] was driven by [driver], not [alternative]* |
| Decomposition | *[Total] is concentrated in [subset]* |
| Trend + implication | *[Metric] [moved] [amount] over [period], [consequence]* |
| Gap | *[Required] but [actual], leaving a [size] gap* |
| Comparative verdict | *[Option] is [verdict] because [criterion]* |
| Threshold breach | *[Metric] will [breach] by [date] unless [condition]* |
| Falsified assumption | *[Belief] does not hold: [evidence]* |
| Recommendation | *[Actor] should [verb] [object] by [date] to [effect]* |
| Counter-intuitive | *Despite [expected], [actual]* |

The **"X, not Y"** construction recurs in strong titles — naming the rejected alternative is
what makes a claim falsifiable and non-obvious.

## Three levels, three registers (house policy)

| Level | Register |
|---|---|
| Presentation title | So-what, SMART, present participle: "Restoring the USPS to Profitability by 2020" |
| Slide title (action title) | So-what — the claim this slide proves |
| **Chart title** | **So-what** (decision #2): what this exhibit specifically shows — narrower than the slide claim. **If word-for-word identical to the action title, drop the chart title.** |

**Framing line** (optional, under the action title, ~10–15 words, grey): how we know — "Based
on n=240 customer interviews", "Excludes one-time charges". Title says what's true; framing
line says how we know. Use it to keep the title a single clean proposition.

## Writing procedure

1. Storyline first — a title is a leaf of a structure.
2. Say the claim out loud, informally. 3. Cut to one proposition. 4. Insert the number or the
noun (neither exists → does the slide have evidence at all?). 5. End-product test if it's an
action. 6. Run the rules. 7. Read it in the ladder — previous title's question answered? next
one raised? "therefore/but" insertable? 8. Does it ladder to the governing thought? No → cut
the slide.

## Defect vocabulary — name it, don't say "weak"

| Defect | Signature | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Topic label | Noun phrase, no verb | Ask "what about it?" |
| Blank assertion | "There are N…", "Overview of…" | **Upstream — more thinking, never rewording** |
| Blank verb | strengthen, improve, review, optimise | End-product test |
| Unquantified | "significant", "strong" | Insert the number |
| Compound | Two propositions joined by "and" | Split the slide |
| Process narration | "We analysed…" | State what was found |
| General truth | True of any company, any year | Find the real claim or cut |
| Absolute | Unqualified all/every/no | Bound it |
| Overclaim | Title asserts more than the exhibit proves | **Claim defect — escalate, never auto-fix** |
| Slop | leverage, unlock, journey… | Say it plainly |
| Orphan | Supports no Key Line point | Cut it or fix the pyramid |
| Non-sequitur | Only "and then" connects neighbours | Storyline defect — route upstream |
