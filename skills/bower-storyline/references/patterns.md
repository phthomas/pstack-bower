# The Seven Storyline Patterns — selector and rules

Policy-resolved distillate. Full research: `centralized_md_references/01-storyline/`.

## The selector — one question decides the family

```
Does the audience need to know WHY, or HOW, or WHAT — just one of them?
   ├─ YES, one  ────────► GROUPING   (Action Jackson · The Pitch · Traffic Light)
   └─ Both why AND how ─► DEDUCTIVE  (Close the Gap · Houston · To B or Not to B · Watch Out)
```

Then pick by situation:

| Situation | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Already agreed; they just need the plan | **Action Jackson** |
| Sell an idea; no plan needed yet | **The Pitch** |
| Reporting status | **Traffic Light** |
| Known target, we fall short, close it — buy-in in one meeting | **Close the Gap** |
| A problem they may not accept + its cause + the fix, one sitting | **Houston, We Have a Problem** |
| Several options; recommend one; they don't know the landscape | **To B or Not to B** |
| Going well, but risks are emerging and action is needed | **Watch Out** |

**Never force-fit.** If no pattern honestly fits, build from the pyramid rules directly and say
so. A pattern that sacrifices logical integrity is worse than no pattern.

## Grouping patterns

**Shape:** So what on top; 2–5 parallel supporting ideas; each supported in turn. Robust.

### 1. Action Jackson — action plans
- CTQ reminds them what was agreed and leads to a **how** question.
- So what is a statement: "We should do X" / "We are implementing a 3-month plan to deliver Y".
- **Every supporting idea is an action starting with a verb.**
- Do NOT use if they still need convincing (→ Pitch, Close the Gap, or Houston).

### 2. The Pitch — pitches and proposals
- Context names **their problem, concretely** ("customers wait 5–7 days despite a 2-hour DC"),
  not generically ("customers complain about delivery").
- So what = the value proposition; must give confidence you can fix it.
- 3–5 reasons, **MECE from the audience's perspective**, their biggest benefit first.
- Trap: glibness. Explain why *they* benefit, never why you do.

### 3. Traffic Light — updates
- Every update resolves to red/amber/green; the So what is the overall judgement, nuanced.
- Elements + status + why underneath.
- Traps: assuming what they know · cut-and-paste from last time · plonking reasons down without
  checking the set is complete and intelligently ordered.

## Deductive patterns

**Shape:** STATEMENT → COMMENT → THEREFORE. Powerful, fragile — break one link, lose it all.

**Universal rules (all four):**
1. The supporting ideas must lead to **only one possible recommendation**.
2. Each does its job: **Statement** = new foundation (conditions / problem / criteria).
   **Comment** = comments on the statement, *tightly* — usually "however"/"but"; weak link =
   dead storyline. **Therefore** = the only natural action if both hold.
3. Each top-level idea parents a **MECE grouping — never another deductive chain.**
4. No padding; every idea earns its place.
5. Storyline deduction drives to an **action**, not a proof: "all members must have beards;
   Socrates is clean-shaven and wants to join; therefore Socrates must grow a beard."

### 4. Close the Gap — improvement recommendations
- S: what success requires (the criteria/standard/target) · C: where we stand (the gap) ·
  T: how we close it.
- Failure modes: incomplete criteria in S (whole story falls) · C not tight to S (audience
  debates you and never reaches the plan) · T not obviously flowing (have someone check).

### 5. Houston, We Have a Problem — problem + cause + fix
- S: the problem, and that it is serious · C: **THE** cause(s) · T: actions **addressing the
  causes named in C**.
- The So what carries BOTH what and why: "We need Marketing to provide the latest sales data
  *to finish the model*." Either half alone fails.
- Do NOT use if they already know the problem and cause ("don't tell me what I already know"),
  or if the audience *is* the cause.
- If it's only *a* cause, not *the* cause, the Therefore doesn't follow — fix the analysis.

### 6. To B or Not to B — option selection
- S: the options — **complete and mutually exclusive** · C: against one shared criteria set,
  B is best · T: implement B.
- So what is a recommendation WITH the why — never "there are three options" (blank assertion).
- Sub-headings are verdicts, not labels: "Option 1 – Outsourcing to Yellow is possible but
  risky", never "Option 1 – Yellow Company".
- CTQ introduces nothing that itself needs justifying.

### 7. Watch Out — emerging risks
- S: what's working / the wins · C: risks that **must** be managed or the outcome breaks ·
  T: actions on those risks.
- The comment carries the pattern: without the imperative they're "any old risks" and no
  action follows. The CTQ question must be about *action*, not just an update.

## The pyramid underneath (always)

- **Three rules:** every parent *summarizes* its children (never labels them) · every group is
  one kind of idea, nameable with **one plural noun** · every group is ordered by a nameable
  order (deductive / chronological / structural / comparative).
- **Key Line:** 2–5 points. One child is not a group; six means the real grouping is missing.
- **SCQA/CTQ is the introduction — one slide, at most two. Never the deck's architecture.**
  The body is governing thought → Key Line → support. The answer is on slide 2.
- **Deduce at the top if you must; group underneath. Never stack deduction on deduction.**
